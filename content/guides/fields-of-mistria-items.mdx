# Fields of Mistria Items: A Source-Based Guide

**Fields of Mistria items** cover the things you collect, craft, equip, cook, donate, place, and use while restoring the town and running a farm. The most practical first step is to identify an item’s category and then check whether it belongs to farming, fishing, foraging, mining, ranching, crafting, furniture, or a museum collection. Use the linked item-specific source and current in-game record for prices, recipes, drop rates, and availability.

**Version note:** Item availability, shop stock, recipes, and perk effects can change between releases. The cited source and current Almanac or shop screen should take priority for version-sensitive details.

## Start with the item category

The community database at [mistria.info](https://mistria.info/) divides its item records into Artifact, Blacksmithing, Bug, Cooked dish, Crops and forage, Fish, Furniture, Material, Milling, Mines, Monster drops, Ranching, and Misc. It also links the item database with related sections for characters, stores, crops, animals, mine dungeons, monsters, skills, and perks. That structure is useful because an item question is often really a location, season, shop, crafting, or collection question.

The [Items page on the Fields of Mistria Wiki](https://fields-of-mistria.fandom.com/wiki/Items) presents another useful split. Its almanac headings include artifacts, blacksmithing, bugs, cooked dishes, crops, fishing and diving, forageables, furniture, materials, and ranching. The same source separately groups equipment, farm supplies, treasure boxes, crafting scrolls, recipe scrolls, bag upgrades, Stamina Ups, and Heart Crystals.

These headings are navigation aids, not proof that every listed object is currently obtainable in every version. When you are looking for a particular object, begin with the broad category, then confirm its own entry or an official update. Do not infer an acquisition method from a similar item when the individual entry does not provide one.

## Tools, equipment, and farm supplies

Tools are items, but they serve a different role from sellable collectibles. The collected 1.0 update notes name an Engagement Ring that can be crafted at a Forge, NPC Special Items from 10-Heart Proposal Events, and a Mystical Feather described there as a family-planning item. They also list Mistril Armor and Tools as the highest equipment tier in that source.

The same update material mentions functional objects such as an Apiary, a Terrarium, a placeable Forge, a Mini-Mill, a Seed Maker, an Artifact Replicator, an Auto-Feeder, a Perpetual Soup Pot, a Tesserae Tree, and a Fish Trap. The stated functions are specific: the Apiary produces Honey; the Terrarium produces Fish Bait and Bug Pheromones; the Mini-Mill has zero milling time; the Seed Maker produces one to three seeds per crop; and the Artifact Replicator turns artifacts into furniture. The cited excerpt does not establish every requirement, unlock, or cost.

Some furniture is functional too. The Wiki item material describes a Big Bell as an object that calls animals in and out of their buildings at once. It describes an Animal Sprite Statue as placeable in a Large Barn or Coop and says it can automatically pet animals after an Essence Stone is added. Keep the exact building restrictions and materials tied to their cited entry, because system behavior may change between versions.

## Crops, forageables, and cooking inputs

The collected Steam community museum guide organizes crops and flora by season. It lists cabbage, cherry, potato, strawberry, and turnip under spring; chili pepper, corn, cucumber, tomato, and watermelon under summer; apple, broccoli, cranberry, pumpkin, and sweet potato under fall; and beet, cauliflower, daikon radish, pomegranate, and snow peas under winter. The guide also includes flowers, herbs, mushrooms, and other forageables in those seasonal groups.

This does not mean every plant in a seasonal list is obtained in exactly the same way. A crop, a wild forageable, and an ingredient sold by a vendor can share a season while requiring different actions. Use a plant’s individual page, a reliable guide, or the in-game record when the question is about seed source, growth time, price, or a recipe ingredient.

The collected player tips video says that cooking can turn ingredients into more valuable products, using an example sequence of wheat, flour, and bread. It also mentions cooking-related perks, but perk names and effects should be checked against the current game version before being used as fixed advice. The broad takeaway is supported: cooking is one item pathway among farming, foraging, and selling, not a substitute for confirming each recipe’s ingredients and output.

## Fish, diving finds, and aquatic items

The Wiki’s item list separates ocean, pond, river, and Deep Woods catches, and it records seasons or weather for many individual fish. Examples in the supplied text include Lake Trout in the Deep Woods in all seasons, Anchovy in the ocean during spring, Koi in ponds in all seasons, and Crayfish in rivers in all seasons. These examples show why a fish guide needs the specific water type and conditions, rather than a generic statement that a fish is “found in Mistria.”

The source also lists shells, clams, seaweed, treasure boxes, and amphibious catches alongside fish. Diving and fishing should therefore be treated as item-finding systems with overlapping but distinct results. The cited list does not supply exact spawn chances or time windows for every entry.

The update notes additionally describe a Fish Trap that produces unique fish and furniture sets. That establishes that the item exists in the cited material, but the note does not provide its full output list.

## Artifacts, mines, and museum collections

Artifact information in the collected sources is location-based. The Steam community museum guide lists dig-spot finds such as Clay, Peat, Shard Mass, Shards, and Sod, then provides separate groups for the beach, Western Ruins, Sweetwater Farm, Eastern Road, The Narrows, Deep Woods, mine floors, and mining nodes. This is a strong reason to organize an artifact guide by where players search, not only alphabetically.

The source also connects several item pools to perks, including Well Placed, Aquatic Antiques, and Sunken Secrets. A perk-related item list should be read as conditional: it does not prove that the item can be found without the named perk, and the source does not provide every probability.

Museum-related choices also deserve care. A community spreadsheet in the collected materials tracks artifacts, fish, forageables, bugs, crops, ranching, and gift preferences, and it advises donating artifacts before selling them when they are hard to replace. That is player guidance rather than official policy, so present it as a community recommendation and not as a guaranteed optimal rule.

## Furniture, cosmetics, and special items

Furniture is a large category in the community database, and the collected update notes mention several named sets: Monster, Ornate, Bonsai, and Dragon. Those notes also mention Crystal Resonators, Song Crystals, a Big Chicken Mount, wedding outfits, a Barrel Hat, Beetle Mandible Horns, Dragonfly Wings, and pet skins. The material says pet skins can drop from monsters through the Friend-Shaped perk.

These names are useful for discovery, but they are not enough to state an acquisition route for every cosmetic or furnishing. Follow the named entry or a current item-specific source rather than turning a partial patch-note mention into an invented collection checklist.

## A reliable way to research any item

First, search the item database or almanac category to establish the correct item name and type. Second, use a location-specific guide, official update, or primary game page to verify how it is obtained; community discussions and videos can help narrow the search, but they should not silently replace a stronger source. Third, record the source URL beside the fact so later updates can be checked.

Keep a claim narrow when the evidence is narrow. “The cited source lists this item in the Deep Woods” is supportable; “this is the best way to farm it” is not, unless the material directly establishes that conclusion. This approach makes **Fields of Mistria items** references more useful because readers can distinguish a sourced location from a player strategy.
